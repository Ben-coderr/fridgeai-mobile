# 90-Second Demo Video — Script

Structure suggested by both the product brief (§31–32) and the hackathon FAQ. Not a mandatory template, but a solid default.

## Before recording: prep the fridge photo

Use a **real photo** (not a stock image) containing recognizable ingredients, per the brief's demo scenario:

> Eggs, tomatoes, chicken, milk, cheese, pasta, vegetables

Take it so that a generated recipe will plausibly have **1–3 missing ingredients** — that's what makes the shopping-list step land. If the AI detects everything perfectly and nothing is missing, the "wow moment" from brief §32 falls flat. Test this ahead of time with the real ingredient list, not guesswork.

## Shot list (target: exactly ~90 seconds)

| Time | Beat | What's on screen |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00–0:15 | The problem | A person opening a fridge, looking unsure what to cook. One line of narration or on-screen text: "What can I cook with what I have?" |
| 0:15–0:65 (50s) | The core feature, working | Screen recording, start to finish: photo → detected ingredients → confirm → preferences → Generate Recipes → recipe list → recipe detail (have/missing) → shopping list. This is the longest and most important segment — don't rush it, but don't narrate every tap either. |
| 0:65–0:80 (15s) | The AI contribution | State plainly: "We use [Gemini] to both see what's in the fridge and generate recipes around it." Show or mention the two-stage architecture (vision → generation) briefly. |
| 0:80–0:90 (10s) | Limitation + next step | One honest limitation (e.g. "ingredient detection is stronger with clear, well-lit photos") and one next step (e.g. "next: dietary restriction support"). |

## Recording tips

- Screen-record the phone (or simulator) directly rather than filming a phone with a second camera — much cleaner for reviewers.
- Record a few takes; keep the one where the AI calls actually succeed (i.e. don't demo network flakiness).
- Label anything simulated or mocked honestly, on-screen or in narration — the rubric explicitly rewards honesty about what's real vs. mocked, and it protects you if a judge asks a follow-up question live.
